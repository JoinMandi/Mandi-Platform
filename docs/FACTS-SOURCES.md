# Symptom facts — evidence verification record

_Date: 2026-08-18. Scope: the nine `questionnaire.facts.*` strings shown in the exhibition results story, the DomainTransition interstitials, and the exhibition results email. Each claim was verified against published literature (all citations confirmed to exist). This record is for Dr Funeka Bango's clinical review — the strings remain flagged in `public/locales/CLINICAL-REVIEW.md` and this table shows the evidence basis per claim._

Framework provenance note: the questionnaire structure derives from MenoScale (ZOE/King's College London, 2025, trial NCT06800560) and the Newson Research Symptom Questionnaire (2025), used **as reference**; per `brief/Team5_MenoScreen_Clinical_Framework_Brief.docx`, formal permissions are to be sought post-funding.

## Verdicts

| Key | Verdict | Evidence | Cite |
|---|---|---|---|
| context | **REWORDED** — "4 to 10 years" → "most often around 4 to 8 years, shorter or longer for some" | STRAW+10 staging puts the transition typically at 4–8 years; the original 4–10 range was not citable. "Menopause is one day" is definitionally correct (final menstrual period). | 1, 2 |
| mind-cognition | **SUPPORTED** as written | Brain fog 40–60% of midlife women (IMS white paper); SWAN (Greendale 2009): perimenopausal cognitive decrements are transient and rebound postmenopause — "for most women, it lifts" holds. Newson 2025 clinic data (anchor): brain fog/memory 90–94%. | 3, 4, 13 |
| mood | **REWORDED** — deterministic "when it dips, mood follows" → "interacts with… mood can shift too" | Estradiol modulates serotonin synthesis/receptors/transporters (review evidence); fluctuation is a risk factor, not a guarantee — causality softened to association. | 5 |
| nervous-system | **SUPPORTED** as written | Avis 2015 (JAMA Intern Med, SWAN): verbatim "Up to 80% of women experience VMS"; onset commonly pre/early perimenopause; median duration 7.4 years. "Up to 8 in 10" is an exact match. | 6, 7 |
| body | **SUPPORTED** as written | Wright 2024 (Climacteric), "musculoskeletal syndrome of menopause": >70% experience musculoskeletal symptoms; frozen shoulder named the top associated complaint; "under-recognised" is the paper's own framing. | 8 |
| urogenital | **SUPPORTED** as written | NAMS 2020 GSM position statement: GSM "is progressive and does not remit spontaneously"; effective treatments exist. Claim mirrors the guideline. | 9 |
| other | **REWORDED** — "far more common than people think" removed | No peer-reviewed prevalence data for alcohol intolerance / new-onset allergy at menopause. The oestrogen–histamine/mast-cell interaction is documented (review), so the reworded copy claims only the hedged mechanism. | 10 |
| quality-of-life | **SUPPORTED** as written | Occupational study (O'Neill 2023): work performance affected 65%, sleep 47%, fatigue 54%; NICE NG23 frames symptom-impact discussion as the entry to care. | 11, 12 |
| mindset | **REWORDED** — blanket "lasting improvement years later" → GSM-specific "some symptoms respond well to treatment no matter how many years have passed" | The broad claim collides with the HRT timing hypothesis (NICE NG23: late systemic HRT initiation has a less favourable balance). GSM treatment effectiveness at any time is solid (NAMS 2020). | 9, 12 |

**Summary: 5 supported, 4 reworded, 0 cut.**
